Surface Topography

(Characterization)

This project characterizes the 3D surface topography of laser-cladded SS316L surfaces after ball burnishing and belt finishing using non-contact measurement techniques. The study evaluates roughness parameters (Ra, Rz, Rpk, Rvk) and bearing area properties with and without Gaussian filtering. Tools and techniques include confocal microscopy, focus variation microscopy, and MountainsMap software for surface analysis. Results revealed distinct surface characteristics, with ball burnishing creating plastic deformation tracks and belt finishing removing peaks while preserving valleys, providing valuable insights into functional surface performance.

View Summary

Powder Characterization

This project compares gas atomized non-spheroidized (NS) and plasma-spheroidized (S) Ti-6Al-4V powders to assess the effect of spheroidization on powder performance. The study evaluates flowability, apparent and tap density, particle morphology, size distribution, shape uniformity, and chemical composition for additive manufacturing. Tools and methods include the Hall funnel (ASTM B213), tapping apparatus (ASTM B527), optical microscopy, SEM imaging, EDS analysis, and Python for data visualization. Results showed that spheroidized powder offers better flowability, higher density, improved packing efficiency, and greater particle sphericity and uniformity.

View Summary

Laser Cladding

This project investigates laser cladding (LC) of SS316L powder on a 42CrMo4 substrate and compares it with cold spray (CS) deposition. The study evaluates powder feed rate, track overlap, clad geometry, microstructure, dilution, hardness, and coating quality. Tools used include laser cladding, cold spray, optical microscopy, SEM imaging, EDS mapping, Vickers microhardness testing, and Python for data analysis. Results showed that optimized LC parameters achieved strong metallurgical bonding, low dilution, and higher hardness than the substrate.

View Summary

Belt Finishing

This project investigates belt finishing of laser-cladded SS316L surfaces using 40 µm and 80 µm abrasive grains. The study evaluates the effects of finishing time and grit size on surface roughness (Ra, Rz) and bearing parameters (Rpk, Rk, Rvk) to achieve superfinished surfaces. Tools and techniques include CNC turning, belt finishing, contact profilometry, optical microscopy, and data analysis. Results showed substantial roughness reduction, particularly within the first 10–20 seconds, with finer grit producing better surface quality and bearing performance.

View Summary

Ball Burnishing

This project investigates ball burnishing as a post-processing method for laser-cladded SS316L surfaces and bulk material. The study evaluates the effect of feed rate on surface roughness (Ra, Rz) and bearing parameters (Rpk, Rk, Rvk) after turning and burnishing. Tools and techniques include a CNC lathe with an Ecoroll tool, contact profilometry, and data analysis. Results showed that ball burnishing significantly reduced roughness, with the best improvements at moderate feed rates, while very low feeds produced irregular results on cladded surfaces due to material non-homogeneity.
